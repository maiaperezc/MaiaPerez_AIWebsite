How it works

Data

Fact AI learns from text scraped from the internet, which can include personal details. Researchers got GPT-2 to repeat hundreds of training examples word for word, including real names, phone numbers and email addresses. [4]

My view So anything personal on the open web could end up in a model.

Patterns

Fact Models find statistical patterns; they don’t look facts up. Their skills are “jagged”: strong at hard tasks, shaky at simple ones, still prone to false statements. [1]

My view It can sound completely sure and still be wrong.

Compute

Fact Every answer is calculated in a data center: racks of chips, cooling and a power bill. [2]

Answer

Fact The output reaches a person or program. Text arrives as a draft; images, voices and video can arrive as convincing fakes. In one study, listeners mistook AI voices for real speakers 80% of the time. [1]

My view This is where a model’s guess becomes someone’s decision.

Check

Fact The last station is a person. The AI Safety Report finds no single safeguard is enough and recommends layers, including human oversight. [1]

My view AI will get things wrong. What matters is whether someone checks.

What it gives us

Range

Fact General-purpose AI can converse fluently in many languages, write code, create images and solve graduate-level science problems. [1]

Study

Fact In a Harvard physics trial, a custom AI tutor more than doubled learning gains versus an active-learning class, in less time. [6]

My view It’s good at explaining things, but I still check the answer myself.

Reach

Fact AI has spread faster than the personal computer: at least 700 million people use leading systems weekly, though likely under 10% across much of Africa, Asia and Latin America. [1]

My view The more people rely on it, the more it hurts when it fails.

Work

Fact In a study of over 5,000 support agents, an AI assistant raised issues resolved per hour by about 15%. Gains went mostly to newer staff; the most experienced saw small quality dips. [5]

Health

Fact In a Swedish trial of about 106,000 women, AI-supported mammography screening detected 29% more cancers and cut radiologists’ reading workload by 44%, without more false positives. Radiologists still read the scans. [7]

My view The best benefit keeps a person in charge.

What goes wrong

Bias

Fact Models perform worse where training data is thin: one study found 79% accuracy on questions about US culture but 12% on Ethiopian culture. [1]

Over-trust

Fact People over-trust, too: in an experiment with 2,784 participants, they were less likely to correct a wrong AI suggestion when fixing it took more effort. [1]

My view The weak link is often the human who stopped checking.

Outages

Fact In October 2025, a race condition in Amazon’s automated DNS system left a key database endpoint empty; dependent services failed for hours. [9]

My view Not an AI bug: a lesson in dependence.

Power

Fact Data centers used about 1.5% of the world’s electricity in 2024. The IEA projects demand will more than double by 2030, with AI the biggest driver. [2]

Fakes

Fact In 2024, a finance worker at engineering firm Arup joined a video call with an apparent CFO and colleagues. Police said the others were deepfakes; about US$25.6 million left in 15 transfers. [8]

My view Confirm money requests on a second channel you already know.

Jobs

Fact Studies find no overall employment effect yet, but falling employment for early-career workers in the most AI-exposed jobs, like software engineering. [1]

My view Entry-level work may feel it first.

Under construction

Agents

Fact Agents are AI systems that act, not just answer. Humans get fewer chances to step in when one fails, and the length of software tasks agents can finish has doubled about every seven months. [1]

Prediction If that continues, multi-day tasks could be automated by 2030, though that is unclear. [1]

Warning

Fact Some researchers think losing control of AI is a serious possibility, even an extinction risk; others think it implausible. Today’s systems show early warning signs, not those abilities. [1]

My view Real harms now, uncertain ones later.

The takeaway

My view AI’s strengths and weaknesses come from the same place. It predicts what sounds likely, so it drafts fast and still gets things wrong. It reaches millions, so it helps many and fails for many. Use it for a first draft, then check it yourself.
